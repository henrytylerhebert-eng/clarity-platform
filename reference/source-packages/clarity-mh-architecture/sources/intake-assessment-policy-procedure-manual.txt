1. Introduction
This manual serves as a comprehensive guide for healthcare providers involved in the intake and assessment process. It aims to standardize procedures, ensuring that each client receives a high level of care that is both ethical and compliant with federal and state laws. The manual outlines specific objectives, procedures, and customer service scripting for each phase of the assessment process.
The importance of a standardized approach cannot be overstated. It ensures that all staff members are aligned in their practices, leading to more accurate assessments and better client outcomes. This manual is intended to be a living document, subject to updates and revisions as healthcare laws and best practices evolve.
Staff members are expected to familiarize themselves with the procedures outlined in this manual and to attend regular training sessions for updates on federal and state laws. Adherence to these guidelines is not just a matter of policy but is crucial for maintaining the high standard of care that our facility is committed to providing.

2. Pre-Assessment Procedures
The pre-assessment phase is critical for setting the tone of the client's experience and for ensuring that the assessment process runs smoothly. This section outlines procedures from client confirmation calls to insurance verification. Each step is designed to minimize disruptions, such as no-shows or incomplete forms, which can compromise the quality of the assessment.
The customer service scripting included in this section serves dual purposes. First, it ensures that clients receive consistent and clear communication from our staff. Second, it helps staff members manage their tasks efficiently. For instance, the script for confirming appointments is designed to both remind the client of their appointment and to provide an opportunity for rescheduling if necessary, thereby reducing the likelihood of no-shows.
Staff members should adhere strictly to the procedures and scripts outlined in this section. Any deviations should be documented and reported to a supervisor for review. This ensures that any potential issues are identified and addressed promptly, maintaining the integrity of the pre-assessment process.





3. During Assessment Procedures
The assessment phase is the core of our service offering. It's during this phase that we gather the information necessary to make a diagnosis and develop a treatment plan. The procedures outlined in this section are designed to facilitate a thorough and accurate assessment while also making the experience as comfortable as possible for the client.
The use of open-ended questions, as specified in the scripting, allows the healthcare provider to gather more nuanced information from the client. This is crucial for making an accurate diagnosis and for developing a treatment plan that is tailored to the client's specific needs. The scripting also includes language for discussing sensitive topics, such as safety concerns, to ensure that this information is gathered in a manner that is both ethical and legally compliant.
Staff members are expected to adhere to these procedures and to use the provided scripting as a guideline. However, it's understood that each client is unique, and some deviation from the script may be necessary to address specific concerns or circumstances. Any such deviations should be documented in the client's medical record.

4. Post-Assessment Procedures
The post-assessment phase is crucial for ensuring that the client receives the appropriate follow-up care and that all relevant parties are informed of the client's treatment plan. This section outlines procedures for sharing case information, conducting follow-up calls, and discussing payment and insurance details. It aims to ensure that the transition from assessment to treatment is as seamless as possible.
The scripting for this phase includes language for discussing potentially sensitive topics, such as payment and insurance coverage, in a manner that is both compassionate and clear. This is crucial for building trust with the client and for ensuring that there are no misunderstandings that could disrupt their treatment plan.
As with the other phases, adherence to these procedures is mandatory. Any deviations should be documented and reported to a supervisor for review. Staff members are also encouraged to provide feedback on these procedures so that they can be updated and improved as necessary.

6. EMTALA and Louisiana Mental Health Code Education
Introduction
Compliance with the Emergency Medical Treatment and Labor Act (EMTALA) and the Louisiana Mental Health Code is not just a legal requirement but a cornerstone for delivering quality healthcare. These laws have a unique impact on the relationship between behavioral hospitals and emergency rooms, particularly in the context of patient referrals. Understanding these laws is crucial for ensuring seamless, ethical, and legally compliant transitions of care between these two types of healthcare facilities.
EMTALA (Emergency Medical Treatment and Labor Act)
Nature of the Law and Its Unique Impact on Referrals
EMTALA mandates that emergency rooms must provide medical screening and stabilization services to all patients, regardless of their ability to pay. While the law is often associated with general emergency care, it also has implications for behavioral health. Emergency rooms cannot "dump" patients by referring them to behavioral hospitals solely because they lack the capability or desire to treat mental health conditions.
Accountability Measures and Referral Protocols
EMTALA holds emergency rooms accountable for ensuring that a patient is medically stable before any transfer to a behavioral hospital. The receiving behavioral hospital, in turn, must have the capacity and capability to treat the patient's condition. Both facilities are required to document that the transfer meets EMTALA criteria, ensuring a legally compliant and medically appropriate referral process.
Louisiana Mental Health Code
Nature of the Law and Its Unique Impact on Referrals
The Louisiana Mental Health Code provides a comprehensive framework for the treatment of mental health patients within the state, including procedures for involuntary commitment. This has a direct impact on how emergency rooms refer patients to behavioral hospitals. For instance, emergency rooms must adhere to the state's specific criteria for emergency certificates and judicial commitments when referring patients for involuntary treatment.
Accountability Measures and Referral Protocols
The Louisiana Mental Health Code holds both emergency rooms and behavioral hospitals accountable for the ethical and legal treatment of mental health patients. This includes ensuring that patients are not involuntarily committed or transferred without due process. The law mandates specific documentation and procedural steps that both types of facilities must follow, thereby standardizing the referral process and ensuring patient rights are upheld.











7. Post-Assessment Test Preview
The Post-Assessment Test is designed to evaluate staff members' understanding and implementation of the procedures outlined in this manual. It covers all phases of the assessment process, from pre-assessment to post-assessment, and includes questions on federal and state laws.
The test is mandatory for all staff members and must be completed annually. It serves as both a refresher on the procedures and as a metric for identifying any areas that may require additional training or clarification.
The test is administered online through the EMR system, and staff members will receive immediate feedback on their performance. Those who do not pass the test on their first attempt will be required to undergo additional training before retaking it.

Conducting the Pre-Screen: A Comprehensive Guide
Pre-Assessment Procedures
1. Client Confirmation:
Objective:
Minimize no-shows and last-minute cancellations.
Procedure:
Call clients 24 to 48 hours before their scheduled assessments.
Script:
"Hello [Client's Name], this is [Your Name] from [Facility Name]. We're looking forward to your assessment scheduled for [Date and Time]. Is that time still convenient for you?"
2. Reception Coordination:
Objective:
Create a welcoming environment.
Procedure:
Share the list of scheduled assessments with the receptionist.
Script:
"Good morning [Receptionist's Name], here are the clients scheduled for assessments today. Please make sure to greet them warmly."
3. Patient Information Form:
Objective:
Collect preliminary information.
Procedure:
Receptionist hands out the Patient Information Form.
Script:
"Welcome to [Facility Name]. Could you please fill out this Patient Information Form? Take your time, and if you have any questions, feel free to ask."
4. Form Review:
Objective:
Ensure completeness and accuracy.
Procedure:
Review the form for completeness.
Script:
"Thank you for filling out the form. I'll quickly review it to make sure we have all the information we need."
5. Preparation:
Objective:
Be well-prepared for the assessment.
Procedure:
Review the Inquiry Form and Patient Information Form.
Script:
(Internal preparation, no script required)



6. Assessment Room:
Objective:
Provide a conducive environment.
Procedure:
Ensure the room is clean.
Script:
"Please make yourself comfortable in our assessment room. Can I offer you some water or coffee?"
7. Clinical Forms:
Objective:
Have necessary forms available.
Procedure:
Prepare a folder with Intake Forms.
Script:
"We'll be filling out a few more forms during our session. Everything is prepared for you."

8. Program Knowledge:
Objective:
Inform the client about programs.
Procedure:
Familiarize yourself with the facility's programs.
Script:
"We offer a variety of programs here that are tailored to individual needs. I'll explain more as we go along."

9. Client Greeting:
Objective:
Make the client feel welcomed.
Procedure:
Greet the client in the lobby.
Script:
"Hello [Client's Name], welcome to [Facility Name]. We're glad you're here. My name is [Your Name], and I'll be conducting your assessment."

Objective:
Decide on family involvement.
Procedure:
Decide whether to assess the family as a unit or individually.
Script:
"Would you prefer for your family to be involved in the assessment, or would you like to proceed individually?"



11. Payment Parameters:
Objective:
Clarify financial aspects.
Procedure:
Discuss healthcare coverage.
Script:
"I understand that understanding healthcare coverage can be complex. Let's go over what your insurance may cover."

Objective:
Confirm insurance coverage.
Procedure:
Verify the client’s insurance.
Script:

During Assessment Procedures
1. Rapport Building:
Objective:
Establish trust and make the client feel comfortable.
Procedure:
Begin with light conversation before diving into the assessment.
Script:
"Thank you for coming in today, [Client's Name]. How was your journey here? Comfortable, I hope?"

2. Questioning Strategy:
Objective:
Gather comprehensive information.
Procedure:
Use open-ended questions to encourage detailed responses.
Script:
"I'd like to understand more about what brings you here. Can you tell me more about your experience?"

3. Problem Identification:
Objective:
Understand the client's perspective on the issue.
Procedure:
Ask the client to describe the problem in their own words.
Script:
"In your own words, could you describe what you feel is the main issue you're facing?"

4. Impact Assessment:
Objective:
Assess the extent of the problem.
Procedure:
Ask how the problem affects different areas of the client's life.
Script:
"How has this issue impacted your work, relationships, or general well-being?"

5. Safety Measures:
Objective:
Ensure the client is not a danger to themselves or others.
Procedure:
Ask direct questions about safety concerns.
Script:
"I need to ask some important questions to ensure your safety and the safety of others. Have you had any thoughts of harming yourself or others?"

6. Chief Complaint: Based on the symptoms described 
Objective:
Identify the nature of the problem.
Procedure:
Summarize the information gathered to form a preliminary diagnosis.
Script:
"Based on what we've discussed, it seems like you're experiencing [Preliminary Diagnosis]. Does that resonate with you?"

7. Treatment Recommendation:
Objective:
Provide a tailored treatment plan.
Procedure:
Suggest treatment options based on the diagnosis.
Script:
"Given what we've talked about, I would recommend [Treatment Plan]. How does that sound to you?"

8. Client Acceptance:
Objective:
Ensure the client is comfortable with the treatment plan.
Procedure:
Address any concerns or barriers the client may have.
Script:
"Do you have any concerns or questions about the treatment plan we've discussed?"

9. External Coordination:
Objective:
Coordinate with other healthcare providers if necessary.
Procedure:
Discuss the need for external coordination with the client.
Script:
"In some cases, we coordinate with other healthcare providers for a more holistic treatment. Would you be comfortable with that?"

10. Summary and Plan:
Objective:
Ensure the client understands the next steps.
Procedure:
Summarize the assessment and the agreed-upon action plan.
Script:
"To summarize, we've identified [Diagnosis] and are considering [Treatment Plan]. The next steps are [Action Plan]. Does everything sound okay to you?"

Introduction
This comprehensive inpatient psychiatric assessment aims to guide healthcare professionals in evaluating patients for inpatient hospitalization. The assessment is designed to establish medical necessity, which is crucial for insurance coverage and ethical treatment. Below each question, you'll find sample questions that build rapport, as well as the types of answers that would be appropriate to document to justify medical necessity.

Section 1: Identifying Information
Patient's Name:
Rapport-Building Question: "May I have your full name, please?"
Type of Answer: "John Doe"
Date of Birth:
Rapport-Building Question: "Could you please share your date of birth?"
Type of Answer: "01/01/1980"
Gender:
Rapport-Building Question: "How do you identify your gender?"
Type of Answer: "Male"
Insurance Information:
Rapport-Building Question: "Could you provide your insurance details, please?"
Type of Answer: "XYZ Insurance, Policy Number 12345"

Section 2: Presenting Problem
Chief Complaint:
Rapport-Building Question: "What brings you in today?"
Type of Answer: "I've been feeling really down lately, even thinking about ending it all."
Duration of the Problem:
Rapport-Building Question: "How long have you been feeling this way?"
Type of Answer: "It's been getting worse over the past six months."
Severity of Symptoms:
Rapport-Building Question: "On a scale of 1 to 10, how would you rate your symptoms?"
Type of Answer: "I'd say it's a 9. I can barely function."





Section 3: Psychiatric History
Previous Diagnoses:
Rapport-Building Question: "Have you ever been diagnosed with a mental health condition before?"
Type of Answer: "Yes, I was diagnosed with Major Depressive Disorder and Generalized Anxiety Disorder."
Previous Hospitalizations:
Rapport-Building Question: "Have you ever been hospitalized for mental health reasons?"
Type of Answer: "Yes, twice last year for suicidal thoughts."
Medication History:
Rapport-Building Question: "Have you taken any medications for your symptoms?"
Type of Answer: "I've tried a few different antidepressants, but they haven't really helped."

Section 4: Risk Assessment
Suicidal Ideation:
Rapport-Building Question: "I know this is a sensitive topic, but it's important for me to ask: have you had thoughts of harming yourself?"
Type of Answer: "Yes, I've been having suicidal thoughts."
Homicidal Ideation:
Rapport-Building Question: "Have you had thoughts of harming others?"
Type of Answer: "No, I haven't."
Self-Harm Behaviors:
Rapport-Building Question: "Have you engaged in any self-harm behaviors like cutting?"
Type of Answer: "Yes, I've been cutting myself for the past month."

Section 5: Social and Environmental Factors
Support System:
Rapport-Building Question: "Do you have friends or family you can talk to about what you're going through?"
Type of Answer: "Not really, I feel pretty isolated."
Living Situation:
Rapport-Building Question: "Who do you live with?"
Type of Answer: "I live alone."
Occupational Status:
Rapport-Building Question: "Are you currently working or studying?"
Type of Answer: "I lost my job because I couldn't focus."





Section 6: Medical Necessity Justification
This section is for internal documentation and doesn't involve patient interaction. It should summarize the medical necessity for inpatient treatment based on the information gathered during the assessment.
Severity of Psychiatric Symptoms:
"The patient's psychiatric symptoms are severe enough to pose a risk to themselves or others, as evidenced by suicidal ideation and self-harm behaviors."
Failure of Outpatient Treatment:
"The patient has tried multiple medications and outpatient therapy with insufficient improvement, indicating a need for a higher level of care."
Need for Specialized Services:
"Given the patient's history of multiple hospitalizations and treatment failures, specialized inpatient services are medically necessary for accurate diagnosis and intensive treatment."

This comprehensive inpatient psychiatric assessment serves as a guide for healthcare professionals to evaluate the medical necessity for inpatient hospitalization. Proper documentation is crucial for justifying the level of care needed and for ensuring that the patient receives the most appropriate treatment.
