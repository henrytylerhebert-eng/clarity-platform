# Module Architecture

## System modules

### 1. Intake Command Center

The command center is the operational home for all active crisis/intake cases.

Responsibilities:

- Active case queue.
- SLA timers.
- referral-to-screening clock.
- intake-to-admission clock.
- insurance verification clock.
- packet completeness score.
- legal deadline warnings.
- facility response status.
- transport/handoff status.
- escalation alerts.

Primary users:

- Intake Director
- Intake Coordinator
- Lead Intake Clinician
- Business Office Representative
- UR/Case Management
- Transport Coordinator

### 2. Guided Crisis Intake Coach

This module captures the story from a family member, patient, officer, mobile crisis worker, ED, or hospital referral source.

It must capture:

- referral source identity and callback
- current patient location
- presenting crisis
- why now
- danger to self
- danger to others
- grave disability
- altered mental status
- psychosis/mania/severe depression
- substance use/intoxication/withdrawal
- recent stressors
- first episode vs. recurring illness
- sleep/eating patterns
- orientation and reality testing
- willingness to accept help
- family/support availability
- insurance and payer information, if available

Design principle:

> Ask in plain language, store in structured clinical fields.

### 3. Structured Psychiatric Assessment

A formal assessment workbench for intake clinicians and reviewers.

Core sections:

- Presenting problem
- Current crisis timeline
- Precipitating events
- Suicide/self-harm risk
- Homicide/violence risk
- Grave disability
- Mental status exam
- Psychosis/mania/mood symptoms
- Psychiatric history
- Medical history and medical suitability
- Medications and adherence
- Substance use and withdrawal/intoxication screen
- Trauma history
- Functional status
- Family/social/supports
- School/work/legal/custody
- Geriatric cognition/capacity/ADL/IADL
- Child/adolescent developmental/family/school context
- Collateral contacts
- Protective factors
- Lower levels of care considered
- Initial recommendation

### 4. Medical Necessity Workbench

This module maps facts to payer-facing medical necessity domains without copying proprietary criteria.

Evidence domains:

- risk of harm
- functional impairment
- treatment history / failed lower LOC
- environmental stressors
- engagement in care
- grave disability
- need for 24-hour monitoring
- medical suitability
- active treatment need
- collateral corroboration
- missing documentation

Output:

- medical necessity snapshot
- authorization-ready narrative draft
- missing facts checklist
- denial-risk flags
- concurrent review update draft

Language guardrail:

Use: “Documentation supports clinician review for inpatient level of care.”

Avoid: “Patient meets InterQual/MCG criteria.”

### 5. Legal Instrument Layer: OPC / PEC / CEC

This is the Louisiana-first defensible wedge.

Responsibilities:

- legal status determination
- OPC draft/issuance workflow
- PEC draft/attestation workflow
- CEC workflow
- statutory deadline tracking
- certifier role validation
- legal form versioning
- e-signature/attestation
- hash sealing
- custody ledger events
- transmission packet binding
- acceptance/decline receipt

All statutory rules must be configurable. Counsel must validate clocks, trigger events, and attestation language before production enforcement.

### 6. Source Provenance and Collateral Ground Truth

Every important fact must be source-linked.

Sources:

- patient report
- family collateral
- law enforcement
- EMS
- ED staff
- outpatient provider
- prior record
- school
- court
- clinician observation
- lab/vitals/medical record
- payer document
- referral document
- bodycam/media reference

The product should distinguish:

- reported by patient
- reported by collateral
- observed by clinician
- imported from record
- generated by AI draft

### 7. Request-Broadcast Routing

Do not build a stale bed registry first.

Build a live case broadcast:

- match patient needs to facility capabilities
- send a referral packet to eligible facilities
- allow one-click accept, decline, or request-more-info
- log response time and reason codes
- preserve decline history
- route to step-down or lower LOC when inpatient is not appropriate

### 8. Treatment Continuity Layer

After admission, the intake record becomes the baseline.

Responsibilities:

- treatment-team handoff
- collateral follow-up tasks
- care actions
- recovery goals
- life stressors
- concurrent review updates
- discharge/step-down referral
- family/support engagement

### 9. AI/MCP Layer

AI is an assistant, not a decider.

AI may:

- summarize structured facts
- draft medical necessity language
- flag missing documentation
- suggest collateral questions
- prepare referral packet drafts
- mine denial letters for template improvements
- generate weekly treatment-team summaries

AI may not:

- diagnose
- assign final risk level
- determine final LOC
- certify medical necessity
- sign or lock legal instruments
- invent missing facts

### 10. Analytics and Quality

Dashboards should measure:

- referral-to-screening time
- intake-to-admission time
- insurance verification time
- packet completeness
- conversion rate
- facility response time
- decline reasons
- process-related denials
- audit compliance score
- referral partner satisfaction
- patient/family satisfaction
- staff throughput
- lost referral reasons
